# ML lane: quantum-inspired feature augmentation

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Result:** the augmentation **does not work**. It stays at `research` stage,
default off. Reported in full because a negative result that cost this much to
establish is the useful part.

## What was built

`qlab/research/quantum_features.py` — the feature *encodings* used by variational
quantum classifiers, written out as ordinary numpy:

- **`angle`** — the product-state encoding `x → [cos(πx/2), sin(πx/2)]` per
  feature (Stoudenmire & Schwab's tensor-network map). Emits a unit-norm pair
  per feature, so no column dominates the ridge penalty by scale.
- **`zz`** — second-order Pauli-ZZ interaction terms, `(π - x_i)(π - x_j)`, the
  entangling content of the IQP circuit in Havlíček et al.'s quantum kernel.
  Emitted as cos/sin of the phase to stay bounded. `n(n-1)` columns.
- **`angle_zz`** — both.

**The claim is narrow and worth stating plainly.** No circuit is simulated and
none is needed: for a product-state encoding the feature vector is closed form,
and it is the *kernel between two such states* that is expensive quantumly, not
the state. These are structured nonlinear bases a quantum circuit would have
produced, at classical cost. Not a speedup, not an approximation of one, and
nothing here improves by running on hardware.

Two properties made them worth trying here:

1. **Pointwise and stateless.** Each output row is a function of that row alone
   — no fit, no rolling window, no cross-sectional statistic — so an augmented
   feature cannot leak the future into a walk-forward split. Only the *input
   scaling* can, which is why bounds are fitted on each fold's training block
   and passed explicitly to its test block.
2. **Explicit interactions.** Realized vol, turbulence, and dispersion plausibly
   interact, and a linear model cannot see that without being handed the
   product.

Wired into the existing purged walk-forward ridge (`predict_vol_ridge`), which
already had the right machinery: 21-day embargo, expanding folds, inner-fold
alpha search, Spearman IC.

## The result

12 independent synthetic samples, 5 outer folds each, paired against the
unaugmented baseline on the same sample:

| augmentation | cols (n=6) | mean IC | sd | wins vs none | paired t | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 6 | 0.1528 | 0.0878 | — | — | baseline |
| angle | 18 | 0.1060 | 0.0746 | 6/12 | -1.72 | no effect |
| zz | 36 | 0.0435 | 0.0881 | **0/12** | **-4.53** | **hurts** |
| angle_zz | 48 | 0.0369 | 0.0852 | 1/12 | **-4.90** | **hurts** |

`zz` did not beat the baseline on a single one of twelve samples.

The mechanism is not mysterious. Six features become 48 columns, against
expanding folds whose early blocks hold a few hundred rows. The added columns
are near-collinear — cos and sin of scaled versions of the same inputs — and
ridge applies one global alpha to all of them, so the penalty that would control
the augmented columns also over-shrinks the raw ones. Textbook variance
inflation. A group-wise penalty or a kernel formulation might rescue it; a
single-alpha ridge cannot.

## The part worth reading

**The first measurement said the opposite, and it was wrong.**

| augmentation | mean IC (first pass) |
|---|---|
| none | 0.0402 |
| angle | 0.1409 |
| zz | 0.1494 |
| angle_zz | 0.1570 |

A 4× improvement in mean IC and stability from 0.14 to 0.61. Encouraging, and
entirely an artifact.

Running a seed sweep to check robustness produced *identical numbers for every
seed* — which is the tell. `get_prices`'s cache key omitted the seed, so the
first offline call populated the cache and every later call with a different
seed silently got that first panel back. The sweep was twelve copies of one
sample, and the "improvement" was one lucky draw.

This is a research-integrity bug of the worst kind: a seed sweep is precisely
the tool for distinguishing a real result from a fluke, and it was silently
returning a constant. Any result run through it would look perfectly stable,
including a spurious one. It is fixed — synthetic panels now cache under a
seed-keyed entry, `cached_provenance` consults it, and
`test_synthetic_cache_identity_includes_the_seed` guards it.

Two lessons recorded rather than paraphrased away:

- **An implausibly good result deserves the same scrutiny as an implausibly bad
  one.** A 4× IC improvement from a generic feature map on a hard forecasting
  problem should have been treated as evidence of a bug from the first look.
- **Zero variance across seeds is never a strong result.** It is almost always
  a broken sweep.

## Status and admission

- `augmentation` defaults to `"none"`. Nothing changes for existing callers.
- The catalog entry stays at `research` stage. It has not cleared the IC
  admission gate and on this evidence it will not.
- The measurement above is on **synthetic data**. The synthetic generator has a
  cleaner factor structure than real markets, so the baseline's IC of 0.15 is
  optimistic in absolute terms. The *paired comparison* is the finding, and it
  is unlikely to reverse on messier data — variance inflation gets worse with
  noise, not better.

## What would be worth trying next

In descending order of expected value:

1. **Group-wise ridge penalties.** Separate alpha for raw and augmented blocks.
   This directly targets the diagnosed mechanism and is a small change.
2. **A kernel formulation.** The ZZ map's natural home is a kernel, where the
   feature count never materialises. Kernel ridge over ~1900 observations is
   entirely tractable.
3. **Fewer, chosen pairs.** All 15 pairs of 6 features is indiscriminate;
   turbulence×dispersion has a story behind it, `realized_vol_21`×`
   mean_abs_return_63` does not.

What is *not* worth trying is the same map with more data or more folds. The
failure is structural, not statistical.
